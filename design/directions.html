Three directions for a film you can hear

Each direction below is applied to aloud's real screens (the shelf and the player, at 1920 by 1080, inside the Fire TV safe area) and to the Devpost thumbnail. Copy, counts and timings come from the Tears of Steel track on the shelf at commit bab4335 (re-described today); if the track changes, the words in the mocks change and the design does not. Film frames are drawn stand-ins; the real gallery uses Virtual Device screenshots.

Recommendation: Signal. Reasons at the end. Binding rules live in design/DESIGN.md.

What reads as generic today

The current app is better than most: its contrast is deliberate, nothing is said with hue alone, and every setting explains itself in plain words. These are the places it still looks assembled rather than designed, worst first. Measured from tv/src/theme.ts and the screen styles, rendered at 1920 by 1080.

  1. The player is a developer dashboard. The film gets about 745 by 420 pixels, 15% of the screen. The schedule strip, a full-width orange "lens" bar and four 400 px setting cards take the rest, and the lens bar is the brightest object on screen, brighter than the film.
  2. Rounded bordered cards for everything. Eleven surfaces (hero, four tiles, four settings, stage, voice panel) share the same 14 to 16 px radius and 2 px border. Settings get the same visual weight as films.
  3. No typeface, no mark. No font family is set, so the app gets the platform default; the video cards use Avenir Next, so the app and the video do not match. The wordmark is 34 px bold system text, smaller than a tile title.
  4. Small tracked capitals. Every label and eyebrow ("DESCRIBED FILMS", "PRESS SELECT TO PLAY", "WHERE EVERY DESCRIPTION SITS") are 17 px uppercase with 3 to 4 px tracking. That is below the 24 px ten-foot floor, and capitals remove the word shapes low-vision readers rely on.
  5. The timeline fails its own rule. Dialogue #4a3b33 against a silence #070505 is 1.9:1; WCAG asks 3:1 for graphics that carry meaning. The README figure draws dialogue in blue (#3987e5) and says 46 descriptions; the app says 45.
  6. Warm black with a terracotta orange. #ff7a45 on brown-black with tan secondary text is the default "tasteful dark mode" palette. It also means the voice colour, focus and the lens all share one orange.
  7. Developer strings in front of viewers. "qwen3-vl-4b (local, llama.cpp on CPU), read by Kokoro 82M (af_heart)" sits on the shelf hero. The provenance is right; the voice code is not for a viewer.
  8. Inside the overscan. The safe area is 64 by 44 px; the Fire TV margin is 5%, 96 by 54 px at 1080p.
  9. Nothing to show yet. No thumbnail, no README hero image, no gallery screenshots. A judge who scores from images alone currently sees text.
Direction A

Signal

Recommended

Broadcast access vernacular: the black and caption yellow that every audio description and subtitle viewer already knows, set in Atkinson Hyperlegible, the face the Braille Institute drew for low vision. Square edges, focus by inversion, the gap mark as the logo.

Type

Atkinson Hyperlegible Next 400/600/800, Mono for timecodes. Scale 80, 52, 46, 34, 28, 24. Sentence case only.

For this audience

Highest voice contrast of the three (13.6:1). Yellow on black is what AD and caption users already read as "access", so it signals competence, not charity.

Risk

Yellow and black can tip into hazard tape. Kept in check by using yellow only for the voice and leaving 90% of every screen black and ink.

Direction B

Liner notes

Film culture, not software. A monochrome bone and black programme, where aloud's voice is set in italic serif like a line of prose, and there is no accent colour at all: the voice is a typeface, not a hue. Titles are a spoken list, not a poster wall.

Type

Newsreader (italic for every description, roman for titles), Public Sans for controls. The voice is recognisable without colour at all.

For this audience

Treats the films and their viewers as an audience for cinema. Monochrome is safe for every kind of colour blindness by construction.

Risk

A serif italic at 50 px is slower for some low-vision readers than a hyperlegible sans, and a list shelf shows less picture to a sighted judge.

Direction C

Meter

Sound you can see. Studio graphite and a level-meter green: every film carries its own dialogue fingerprint, settings read as meters, and the wide Archivo face gives aloud the confidence of broadcast graphics.

Type

Archivo at 125% width for the wordmark and titles, 100% for text. Tabular figures throughout.

For this audience

Makes the invisible product visible to the sighted half of the sofa and to judges; the fingerprints are real data, so they double as proof.

Risk

Closest to a tool aesthetic, the thing the audit already flags. Green on graphite also sits near the "AI dashboard" look.

Signal is the only direction whose look comes from the audience rather than from us. Atkinson Hyperlegible was drawn for low-vision readers, and black with caption yellow is the colour of access on television already, so a judge reads "this is for blind and low-vision viewers, made with care" before reading a word, and a low-vision viewer gets the highest contrast voice of the three. It also has the smallest Phase 2 risk on a deadline: it changes theme.ts and five components, and leaves the focus model and every behaviour alone.

Two borrowings are worth keeping whichever is chosen: the per-film dialogue fingerprint from Meter (real data, under each tile) and the first-description preview from Liner notes (one real line on the shelf).
